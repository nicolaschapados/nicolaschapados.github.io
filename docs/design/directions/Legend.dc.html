Six directions

Same content on every board: nav (About, Publications, Talks, Blog, EN/FR), a hero, latest post, selected publications, next talk, CV, links. Bracketed text is a placeholder for facts not yet decided. Each direction pushes one axis; pick one, or name what to combine.

A · Quiet Journal
Warm paper, serif headings, small caps, prose-first grid. Academic rigor is the whole look.
Tradeoff: the least "fresh" of the six; can read as a personal homepage from a good university.
B · Swiss Grid
Black on white with one blue accent, strict 12-column grid, a huge sans headline, numbers as design. Professional and modern.
Tradeoff: cool and impersonal; rigor comes from structure rather than typography.
C · Monograph
Dark ink ground, light serif at large size, brass accent, black-and-white portrait. Considered, book-like.
Tradeoff: dark sites read as luxury or product; harder for long blog reading, needs a light mode for posts.
D · Lab Notebook
Grid paper, monospace metadata, cards with hard shadows. The blog and its figures lead; the record supports.
Tradeoff: playful; the least formal for a senior bio, and the easiest to overdo.
E · Bold Type
Name set at poster size, one vivid accent, asymmetric blocks. The most startup-modern.
Tradeoff: personality-forward; academic rigor rests on the serif body and list discipline alone.
F · Index
Sidebar navigation, tabular rows, dates in monospace. The landing is the archive: dense, scannable, data-first.
Tradeoff: utilitarian; no hero moment, little room for a portrait or a statement.
